# Classic encounter progression correction

Subsequent milestone: [enemy AI and expansion](ClassicEnemyAI.md) now adds strategic orders and colony investment around the encounter flow below. The previously documented enemy-investment limitation is removed for initialized Classic games; wander destination indexing is now corrected; other source-undefined states retain explicit boundaries.

## Reproduced failure and root cause

An ordinary D/a/B order followed by G reaches a stationary enemy. `BeginClassicTurn` moves the player fleet to B and sets `ClassicTurnInProgress`. The previous `PrepareClassicCombat` then threw `Automatic fleet combat and withdrawal are not implemented; the turn is pending.` before creating any playable interaction. The CLI caught the exception and returned to movement input. Subsequent G/space called `FinishEconomicTurn`, which retried that same failing combat check; no clock completed, while L and other movement commands remained available. It was a deliberately deferred implementation boundary with an unusable interaction, not a missed clear after an already-resolved battle.

`PendingSharedStartEncounter` is a different guard, set only by startup finalization when the starting fleets share a star. Ordinary arrivals do not set it. Before this fix there was no way to resolve that guard either. It correctly prevents direct/debug ticks from skipping the startup battle; it is retained and now cleared by combat completion rather than by arbitrary movement or an unconditional reset.

The source-derived reproduction in `EncounterProgressionTests.OrdinaryDThenGArrivalAutomaticallyPresentsBattleAndTurnsResumeAfterEnemyDestroyed` failed before the correction: expected completed turn 2, actual 0. It now passes. The fixture issues normal D and G keys to approach the enemy rather than directly setting pending encounter flags. A separate seeded, fully initialized/shared-start fixture exercises the real startup guard.

## Original behavior and implemented correction

References: `OriginalSource/main.c:main`, `init.c:init_player`, `combat.c:battle/tf_battle/withdraw`, `display.c:display_forces`, `movement.c:lose/set_des`, `taskforce.c:get_tf/split_tf/joinsilent`, `input.c:get_stars`, `enemy.c:best_withdraw_plan/enemy_attack`, `misc.c:help2/pause`.

C automatically calls `battle()` after movement, and after investment; shared starts call it before the first movement prompt. For each star, fleet battle precedes enemy planetary response and then the optional player planetary attack. Both sides stationary with warships on either side requires a fleet battle, even if one side only has scouts/transports. Unarmed sides can remain co-located without another battle. Fleets merely crossing during travel do not fight.

The player does **not** start an encounter by pressing movement-phase B (which means bombardment). The fleet battle automatically presents forces and `Press any key to continue`. That acknowledgement fires the first exchange. If combat remains, B? accepts G/space for another exchange or W to split/withdraw. M/H/S/T/C/R and O/?, with the original meanings, are available there; H does not add a pause. Movement L/D/Q/B are illegal during B?. When combat ends, a final acknowledgement precedes revolt, subsequent planetary interactions and clock completion. Repeated G/space therefore progress within the mandatory interaction and then return to the ordinary turn loop; they do not silently retry an unimplemented boundary.

The correction uses transient Core sessions and the existing Classic terminal/input methods, without a replacement input system:

- `Game.Encounters.cs` sequences stars and fleet/enemy/player operations. `FleetBattle` stores the initial pause, B? orders, final pause, losses, odds and pending withdrawal selections. Enemy planetary responses use the existing planetary salvo rules, parameterized by attacking team, with the original local target scoring and pre/post-salvo pauses. No strategic movement, colony spending or new economic rules are added.
- Fleet strength, transport/scout defense, probabilities, casualty order and repeated rounds follow C. Damage rolls enemy T/S/C/B then player T/S/C/B. The first round runs once; later rounds repeat until either side loses something. Probabilities use `exp(log(.8)*min(opposingForce/(ownForce+T+2*S),14))`, frozen during the exchange. Strict `rnd(100)/100.0 > survival` kills an individual ship. Odds refresh between exchanges. Multiple player fleets merge into a fresh slot as in C.
- The required enemy combat withdrawal occurs before B? input: T/S withdraw when threatened by player warships; warships join them according to `best_withdraw_plan` thresholds. Random destinations are rejection-sampled in original 1..NUM_STARS bounds, using exact positive distance within enemy range. ETA retains C's `(int)((distance-.01)/velocity)+1`; enemy origeta stays zero. Contents remain on the battle parent until the decision is completed, then subtract exactly once. Reports show `en withdraws`.
- W reuses player splitting and existing range/course calculations. Blank transfers the entire battle fleet; selected ship tokens support partial withdrawal. Invalid, same-star or out-of-range destinations rejoin ships to the battle parent. Success sets withdrew. EOF after splitting retains the destination prompt in Core, so resumption does not split or roll again. Complete player withdrawal ends the fleet exchange after settling any pending enemy withdrawal.
- Empty fleet destinations/references are cleaned up. Surviving ships remain in the proper fleet; a star/fleet/colony refresh follows each change. Final battle acknowledgement applies revolt. Planetary attacks still retain their different first/later salvo rule and protected T/S; the fleet-battle repeat condition is not substituted for it.
- `PrepareSharedStartCombat` resolves the original startup combat pass without moving or advancing the calendar. `PendingSharedStartEncounter` stays set through pauses and clears only when the complete startup pass finishes. Direct/debug tick guards still reject unresolved startup or Classic phases. New Game/loop facades resume the same session/odds/losses rather than replaying movement or combat.
- Economy preparation/completion checks all pending combat, not only P?. The obsolete contested-star production guard is removed: actual combat now runs first, and harmless unarmed co-location is permitted. Due-cycle preflight defers to a possible encounter before testing the existing enemy-economy boundary, so it cannot incorrectly block a battle that would change ownership or destroy fleets. No production formula/cost changes are made.

## Limits and safe handling of C defects

Subsequent milestones implement enemy movement/orders/investment and normal endgame (ClassicEnemyAI.md and ClassicEndgame.md); persistence remains unimplemented. Enemy combat withdrawal and immediate enemy planetary response are necessary parts of the encounter, not a strategic AI milestone. Enemy-owned colonies still explicitly stop a due investment cycle; this economic limitation is distinct from the fixed encounter deadlock and is reported in the status line.

C fails to check fleet-slot exhaustion in battle merging/enemy withdrawal and can write dummy slot zero. The conversion reports the task-force limit and retains the pending battle. Previously resolved casualty rolls are not repeated on retries. W also reports its existing limit without losing ships. If an enemy withdrawal has no other reachable star, C rejection sampling cannot terminate; this conversion reports that condition rather than hanging or inventing a destination. Such unsupported states remain pending with a visible reason; no combat validation is bypassed. Normal reachable configurations preserve sampling and RNG order, including rejected current-star draws.

The UI retains established monochrome/composed-frame/pagination adaptations. Loss text aggregates repeat-pass casualties instead of overlapping C cursor writes. Some invalid W errors use existing Core movement wording, while their rejoin/ship-state behavior matches C. General unsafe C indexing/control-byte echoes are not reproduced. Artificial states with several independent stationary enemy fleets, zero weapons or invalid counts remain outside normal source assumptions; normal enemy arrivals merge. Full original PRNG identity and compiled-original/physical-console comparison remain unestablished; validation uses C source and deterministic literal draw/state/screen fixtures.
