# Classic combat: bombardment and player planetary assaults

The subsequent [enemy expansion milestone](ClassicEnemyAI.md) now issues original enemy orders before movement and runs enemy investment/research. These reuse the combat flow below and the shared blast arithmetic. Strategic wandering now uses the documented deterministic correction to C destination indexing; scoring/endgame now follow ClassicEndgame.md; persistence remains separate.

Source audit: `command.c:blast_planet`, `combat.c:blast/playerattack/play_salvo/fire_salvo/tf_battle/withdraw/battle`, `display.c:display_forces`, `movement.c:lose/movement`, `update.c:revolt/invest`, `enemy.c:enemy_attack/best_withdraw_plan`, `main.c`, and `misc.c:help1/help2/help3/on_board/pause` in `OriginalSource`. B bombardment, P? player planetary assault, and the mandatory fleet-battle B? loop are three different interactions. B? and immediate enemy planetary responses were added by the subsequent [encounter progression correction](ClassicEncounters.md).

## Original command and turn sequence

Movement-phase **B means Blast Planet**, not begin a fleet battle. It has no RNG or clock advancement. The original header is `>>>>>>>> Blast Planet <<<<<<<<` on row 18. Row 19 asks ` Firing TF:` and consumes one fleet letter. Validation is ordered: illegal slot, nonexistent fleet, nonzero ETA (including pending travel), already blasting, no cruisers/battlestars, no planets. Withdrawal alone does not prevent B. A sole planet is selected automatically; otherwise row 20 `Target colony :` consumes one number key. Enemy ownership rejects with ` !Conquer it first!`; an unconquered player colony rejects with ` !That is a human colony!!`. Neutral planets and conquered player colonies are valid, even with another enemy fleet present: B itself does not test enemy presence or initiate an encounter.

Row 21 reads `Units (max   5) :` (three-column maximum), with the quantity cursor at column 18. `get_token`'s first quantity is used: blank/zero cancels; a nonnumeric first token means one; leading digits specify a quantity and the remainder is ignored. Thus `-3` means one, not minus three. Unlike L, blank does **not** mean all. Row 22 reports `Blasting   n units`. Error and completion return to movement input. There is no additional confirmation, Escape rule or turn charge.

The C main loop is player input, enemy orders, movement/exploration/revolt, `battle`, investment on its scheduled year, then annual update and endgame check. `invest` calls `battle` again after all growth/spending. Startup also calls combat for a shared starting location. `battle` visits stars in ascending order, and at each star:

1. Both sides stationary and either has cruisers/battlestars: mandatory `tf_battle`, even against transports/scouts alone. Passing in flight or unarmed fleets alone does not trigger this battle.
2. Enemy warships plus player colonies: enemy planetary attack.
3. Player warships plus enemy colonies: optional player planetary attack.

Core now runs fleet battle, enemy planetary response and player planetary assault in original per-star order after movement/arrival acknowledgements/revolt, and again after due investment. The earlier exception-only fleet/enemy boundary caused the hands-on G/space deadlock and is replaced by playable interactions. Shared-start combat runs before movement and clears its guard only after resolution. See [root cause, exact changes and limits](ClassicEncounters.md).

## Bombardment arithmetic and state

Full force is `playerWeapons * (7 * cruisers + 40 * battlestars)`. The displayed maximum is force / 4 with integer truncation. A positive request uses `min(fullForce, request * 4)` and sets the fleet's blasting flag if force is positive. The reported units are force / 4 **before** limiting destruction to planetary capacity.

`killed = min(oldCapacity, force / 4)`; population becomes `min(oldPopulation, oldCapacity) - killed`; IU becomes `min(oldIU - killed, newPopulation * 2)`; capacity decreases by killed. If population is nonpositive, population, IU, MB and AMB all become zero. If the planet previously had an owner, it becomes neutral, enemy-seen ownership becomes neutral and conquest clears. Already-neutral planets retain stale conquest/enemy-intelligence flags, as in C. Under-attack is untouched. Player-seen capacity updates immediately. No ships, transports, research or money are consumed. Blasting blocks another B and the existing D/J/N paths until movement resets it; original L still allows that fleet to land. Neutralizing a conquered colony can therefore allow landing again if capacity remains.

Safe adaptation: quantities whose multiplication would overflow a C signed int are rejected. C overflow is undefined. Normal gameplay retains its integer divisions and original arithmetic, including surprising IU/population results; no substitute damage model is used.

## Player planetary assault: P?

The header is `>>>>>> Attack at Star A <<<<<<`; the star receives `!`, its report is refreshed, and row 19 asks `P?`. G/space fires a salvo; B means **Break off attack** in this context. S/T/C/R/M reports, J/N and H/pause use their original meanings. D, L, Q, W, ? and other keys are illegal here. Breaking off clears under-attack flags for all planets at the star, restores the map, applies revolt and pauses with `Planet attack concluded` / `Press any key to continue`. It neither moves the fleet nor sets its withdrew flag. This is not fleet withdrawal.

`Attack planet :` consumes one planet key only when more than one enemy colony exists; otherwise the sole enemy colony is automatic, even when there are other neutral/owned planets. Invalid planet gives `! That is not a useable planet`; nonenemy gives ` !Not an enemy colony`. ` attacking tf :` consumes one fleet letter only when multiple stationary player fleets are present, including unarmed fleets. The sole eligible fleet is automatic. Ordered errors are illegal/nonexistent/not-at-star/no-warships. No blasting or withdrawal check is added. A first salvo marks the target under attack, revealing its bases through the existing star-summary visibility rule; other enemy planets' defenses remain hidden.

Attacker force is weapons times `(7*C + 40*B)`; defender force is defender weapons times `(7*MB + 40*AMB)`. With positive defense, attacker survival is `exp(log(.8) * min(defense/attack, 14))`; defender survival swaps the ratio. Ratios are floating point. The UI prints force, weapons and survival percentages, followed by attacker and planet losses. For every individual ship/base, `rnd(100)` is inclusive 1..100; death is **strictly** `draw/100.0 > survival`. Draw order is attacker C, attacker B, planet MB, planet AMB. T/S never roll or die in planetary salvos. Odds remain fixed for the whole salvo invocation, not recalculated after each casualty.

The first salvo at each planet does exactly one loss pass and can cause no losses. The later-salvo loop literally reads `while (!first_time && !p_losses && a_losses)`: flags accumulate across passes, so it repeats while attacker losses have occurred but **no planetary loss** has occurred. It is not a symmetric "fight until somebody loses" loop. It can continue after the selected fleet loses its last warship. A base can subsequently die and a different stationary player warship can claim the planet. Regression fixtures lock down this behavior and exact RNG consumption.

With no remaining bases and **any** stationary attacking warship at that star, ownership and enemy-seen ownership change to the attacker and conquered becomes true. Population, IU, capacity and player-seen capacity are unchanged. No transports are required. An undefended colony falls without any RNG draws. Empty fleets lose their destination and board references; surviving T/S keep a fleet active. Assault continues while enemy colonies and player warships remain. Conquered planets without a guard revolt when the assault ends, potentially undoing earlier captures if the last warship dies. Revolt refreshes player-seen capacity but leaves enemy-seen ownership stale, matching C. Existing investment rules halve conquered growth and prohibit defensive purchases/transports there.

## Fleet battles and immediate enemy combat responses

`tf_battle` merges multiple stationary player fleets into a newly allocated slot, then displays forces and pauses. Its first loss phase occurs before any B? decision. Offensive strengths use weapons and 7/40 guns; survival denominators additionally include the side's own transports * 1 and scouts * 2. It rolls enemy T/S/C/B, then player T/S/C/B. Later phases repeat **until either side has losses**, unlike the asymmetric planetary loop; odds recalculate between phases. Destroyed fleets are cleaned up and unguarded colonies can revolt. Planet population is not bombarded by fleet exchanges.

Before the player's B? decision, C allocates an enemy withdrawal fleet. With player warships present, enemy T/S always withdraw; warships join them if `best_withdraw_plan` accepts. That heuristic uses the largest planet's capacity/owner: survival < .70 with capacity < 30, < .50 with player ownership, < .30 with capacity < 60, or < .20 unconditionally. A random in-range destination is rejection-sampled with `rnd(NUM_STARS)` and its ETA is `(int)((distance-.01)/enemyVelocity)+1`. The enemy's withdrawn contents are removed after the player's decision. Slot allocation is unchecked in C and can use dummy slot zero at exhaustion.

B? supports M/H/S/T/C/R, O (odds), W (withdraw), ? (no-op), G/space (next exchange). These are now implemented. W invokes original splitting and destination prompts; a valid withdrawal sets withdrew, while invalid/out-of-range/same-star destinations rejoin split ships to the battle parent. Initial/final pauses, enemy withdrawal before the player decision and loss/odds order are covered by [encounter regression tests](ClassicEncounters.md). W remains illegal in P?, where B breaks off instead.

`enemy_attack` selects a player colony by a negative score using enemy-seen defense / `(C + 6B)`, adjusted by capacity (subtract 2 if capacity > 30, else 1.5; multiply by capacity). It calls shared planetary salvos with per-planet first-time flags, updates enemy-seen defense to MB+6*AMB, pauses before/after each salvo, repeats while an attractive target and warships remain, then restores map/revolts. This immediate combat response is implemented to complete encounters after fleet losses. Strategic enemy travel/orders/investment now follow ClassicEnemyAI.md; wandering now uses corrected reachable-star indexing; endgame now follows ClassicEndgame.md; persistence remains deferred.

## Rendering, validation and remaining differences

Rules live in `Game.Combat.cs` and `Game.Encounters.cs`; existing fleet helpers rebuild layered references. Planetary/fleet sessions and a per-star combat cursor live in GameState, following the investment pattern. Another Game/Classic loop can resume at EOF without replaying movement, salvos, rolls or acknowledgements. Classic G cannot complete its clock while combat/acknowledgements remain. Debug tick stays movement-only and cannot bypass a pending Classic phase.

Map, star/fleet/colony reports refresh from existing state. The interface remains monochrome. It presents stable composed frames rather than C's cursor writes and transient `#` capture flash; `!` remains throughout an active assault. Repeated-pass casualty text aggregates losses rather than reproducing C's overlapping writes. C's erroneous `%c` echo of the numeric fleet slot (a control byte for A, etc.) is replaced by a readable fleet letter. No control bytes are injected. Existing report pagination, EOF safety and console adaptations remain unchanged. Corrupt zero-weapon/negative-count states and C undefined overflow are not assigned invented gameplay semantics.

`CombatTests` covers force arithmetic, quantities/eligibility, neutral/conquered destruction, population/IU clamping, flags/intelligence quirks, weapon ratios, strict thresholds, exact draw order/counts, first/later salvo asymmetry, T/S survival, capture, helper fleets, cleanup, last-guard revolt, turn order, shared sessions and explicit unsupported encounters. `ClassicCombatTests` covers original prompt rows/cursors, automatic and multiple selectors, token parsing, cancellation/EOF/resumption, errors, battle output, help, break-off, ownership/map/report refresh and unchanged clock until acknowledgement. Existing startup, movement, research, J/N, L, economy and debug-shell suites also run.

Comparison is against inspected C and source-derived deterministic fixtures, not a compiled original executable or a physical-console comparison. Bounded RNG requests/order are preserved; full C PRNG stream equivalence remains unestablished, especially with enemy actions absent.
